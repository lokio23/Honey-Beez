# Session 1: "What IS a Bee?"

**Honey Beez Curriculum | Ages 9-12 | 75 minutes**

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

- Identify at least 5 key anatomical features of a honey bee and explain their functions
- Distinguish between bees, wasps, and other look-alikes using observable traits
- Debunk at least 3 common myths about bees using evidence
- Describe the basic role structure inside a honey bee colony

---

## Materials Needed

### Supplies
- Large craft paper or butcher paper (1 roll)
- Markers, colored pencils, crayons
- Pipe cleaners (yellow, black, brown — 5 per participant)
- Pom-poms in assorted sizes (large for thorax/abdomen, small for eyes)
- Cardstock or foam sheets (yellow, black, clear/white)
- Glue sticks and tape
- Scissors (safety scissors for younger participants)
- Magnifying glasses (1 per pair of participants)
- Printed bee anatomy reference cards (1 per table)
- Small ziplock bags with pollen substitute (turmeric powder works well)
- Branched feathers or fuzzy fabric scraps (to demonstrate branched hairs)

### Printed Materials
- Trading Cards Pack 1 (1 set per participant — Scout Sunny, Nurse Nina, Guard Gary, Queen Bea)
- Bee vs. Wasp comparison sheet (1 per participant)
- Myth vs. Fact cards (1 set per table)
- Bee anatomy labeling worksheet (1 per participant)

### Technology
- Laptop/tablet with projector or large screen
- Virtual Hive Simulator loaded and ready
- Speaker for audio clips

### Facilitator Prep
- Draw or print a large outline of a bee on butcher paper (at least 3 feet long) for the collaborative model
- Pre-cut wing shapes from clear/white cardstock
- Set up tables in groups of 4-5
- Place magnifying glasses and pollen substitute bags at each table

---

## Narrative Through-Line

> Today we receive our first transmission from the Honey Beez Hive. Queen Bea has sent Scout Sunny to find new recruits — that's us. But before we can join the hive, we need to prove we understand what makes a bee... a bee. Scout Sunny has left us a series of challenges. If we pass, we earn our first set of Hive Trading Cards and official recruit status.

## Facilitator Script

### HOOK — "The Sesame Seed Brain" (10 minutes)

**[0:00 - 0:03] Opening Mystery**

*Hold up a sesame seed (or show one on screen).*

> "Everyone see this? This is a sesame seed. Now, I want you to imagine something: there's a creature on this planet with a brain THIS size... and it can count to four. It can add. It can subtract. It can recognize human faces. It can learn from watching other creatures make mistakes. And there are about 2 TRILLION of them on Earth right now."

*Pause for guesses.*

> "We're talking about BEES. And today, we're going to discover that almost everything you think you know about bees... is probably wrong."

**[0:03 - 0:06] The Challenge Letter**

*Read aloud or display on screen:*

> "TRANSMISSION FROM THE HONEY BEEZ HIVE: Greetings, human recruits. I'm Scout Sunny, explorer for Queen Bea's colony. Her Majesty has authorized me to begin recruiting a new class of Hive Allies. But first — do you even know what a bee IS? Most humans don't. They confuse us with wasps. They think we all make honey. Some of them even think we die every winter! Today I'll be testing you. Pass my challenges, and you'll earn your official Hive Trading Cards. Fail... and, well, let's just say Guard Gary won't be happy. — Scout Sunny, Expedition Division"

**[0:06 - 0:10] Quick Poll**

Run a rapid-fire hand-raise poll:

- "How many legs does a bee have?" (6 — some will say 4 or 8)
- "How many eyes does a bee have?" (5 — almost nobody gets this)
- "Do all bees sting?" (No — male bees have no stinger)
- "Do all bees make honey?" (No — only about 5% of bee species make honey)
- "Can bees fly in the rain?" (Yes, but they prefer not to)

> "If you got any of those wrong, GOOD. That means you're about to learn something amazing. Let's go."

---

### EXPLORE — "Bee Anatomy Deep Dive" (20 minutes)

**[0:10 - 0:18] The Five-Eyed Flier**

*Display a high-resolution bee anatomy diagram on screen.*

> "Let's start with something wild. Bees have FIVE eyes. Two big compound eyes on the sides — those are made of thousands of tiny lenses called ommatidia. A single compound eye might have 6,900 individual lenses. But then there are three more tiny eyes on top of the head called ocelli. Those don't see images — they detect light and help the bee know which way is up when it's flying."

*Pass out magnifying glasses. Give each table a printed high-res photo of a bee's head.*

> "Use your magnifying glasses. Can you spot the three ocelli? They look like tiny bumps arranged in a triangle."

*Give tables 2 minutes to examine and discuss.*

**Branched Hairs Demo:**

*Hold up a regular hair or smooth thread, then hold up a branched feather or fuzzy fabric scrap.*

> "Here's another thing that makes bees special. See this smooth thread? That's kind of like a wasp's body hair — smooth. Now look at this branched feather. THAT's what bee hair looks like under a microscope. Every single hair on a bee's body splits into branches, like tiny trees. Why? Because branched hairs are AMAZING at trapping pollen. It's like Velcro for flower dust."

*Let participants feel both textures.*

**[0:18 - 0:22] Pollen Baskets Demonstration**

*Hold up a ziplock bag of turmeric powder.*

> "This is our stand-in for pollen. Now watch..."

*Sprinkle some turmeric on smooth fabric (wasp comparison) — it slides off. Then sprinkle on fuzzy/branched fabric (bee comparison) — it sticks.*

> "THAT's the difference. But bees go even further. They have special flat, concave areas on their back legs called pollen baskets — or corbicula. They actually pack pollen into these baskets, mixed with a tiny bit of nectar to make it sticky, and fly it home. A single bee can carry pollen equal to 30% of its body weight. That'd be like you carrying a golden retriever on your back while running."

**[0:22 - 0:26] Myth Busters Station**

*Distribute Myth vs. Fact cards to each table. Each card has a statement. Tables must sort them into MYTH or FACT piles.*

Statements to include:
1. "All bees live in hives." (MYTH — most bees are solitary)
2. "Bees die after they sting you." (PARTIAL — only honey bees, and only females)
3. "Bees make honey by vomiting." (SORT OF TRUE — they regurgitate nectar, processed in a special honey stomach)
4. "There are over 20,000 species of bees." (FACT)
5. "Wasps and bees are the same thing." (MYTH)
6. "Bumblebees can sting multiple times." (FACT)
7. "Bees are attracted to bright colors." (FACT — especially blue, purple, yellow)
8. "Honey never spoils." (FACT — archaeologists found edible honey in Egyptian tombs)
9. "All bees make honey." (MYTH — only about 5% of species do)
10. "Killer bees are a separate species." (MYTH — they're a subspecies of western honey bee)

*Give tables 4 minutes to sort. Then reveal answers together. Celebrate correct answers and explain the tricky ones.*

**[0:26 - 0:30] Bee vs. Wasp Showdown**

*Display side-by-side images.*

> "Let's settle this once and for all. Bees: fuzzy, thick body, branched hairs, eat pollen and nectar, generally docile. Wasps: smooth, narrow waist, carnivorous (they eat other insects), more aggressive. Both are important! Wasps are amazing pest controllers. But they are NOT the same creature."

*Hand out the Bee vs. Wasp comparison sheet.*

---

### MAKE — "Build a Giant Bee" (25 minutes)

**[0:30 - 0:35] Introduction and Role Assignment**

*Unroll the large butcher paper with the pre-drawn bee outline on a table or floor space.*

> "Scout Sunny's first challenge: build a life-size honey bee — well, a BIG life-size. We're going to label every part. Each team at your table is responsible for one body section."

Assign table groups:
- **Team Head:** Compound eyes, ocelli, antennae, proboscis (tongue), mandibles
- **Team Thorax:** 6 legs (with pollen baskets on the back pair), 4 wings, flight muscles
- **Team Abdomen:** Honey stomach, wax glands, stinger (with barbs), breathing holes (spiracles)
- **Team Details:** Branched hairs all over, color patterns, label cards

*Each team gets relevant craft supplies and a reference card showing their section.*

**[0:35 - 0:50] Build Phase**

*Circulate among tables. Use guiding questions:*

- "How many segments does the antenna have? Check your reference card."
- "Where do the pollen baskets go? Which pair of legs?"
- "The stinger has barbs — how can you show that with your materials?"
- "Don't forget the spiracles — those are breathing holes along the sides of the abdomen."

*Play ambient nature/bee sounds quietly in the background.*

**[0:50 - 0:55] Assembly and Presentation**

*Have teams attach their sections to the large bee. Each team presents their section (30-45 seconds each):*

> "Team Head, tell us one amazing thing about the part you built."

*After all teams present, take a group photo with the completed bee.*

> "Scout Sunny would be impressed. You've just built a scientifically accurate bee model. This stays up on our wall for the rest of the program."

---

### PLAY — "Bee Trivia Tournament" (15 minutes)

**[0:55 - 0:57] Setup**

> "Guard Gary doesn't let just anyone into the hive. He's set up a trivia challenge. Tables are teams. I'll ask questions. Discuss with your team quietly, then hold up your answer card when I say GO."

*Give each table a set of A/B/C/D cards or whiteboards.*

**[0:57 - 1:07] Trivia Rounds**

**Round 1 — Quick Fire (1 point each):**
1. How many eyes does a bee have? (A: 2, B: 3, C: 5, D: 8) **Answer: C**
2. What is the name for a bee's pollen-carrying leg structure? (A: Pollen pouch, B: Corbicula, C: Pollen purse, D: Leg basket) **Answer: B**
3. Approximately how many bee species exist? (A: 500, B: 2,000, C: 20,000, D: 200,000) **Answer: C**
4. What shape are the cells in a honeycomb? (A: Circle, B: Square, C: Triangle, D: Hexagon) **Answer: D**
5. Which bee in the hive is male? (A: Worker, B: Queen, C: Drone, D: Guard) **Answer: C**

**Round 2 — True or False (2 points each):**
6. A queen bee can lay up to 2,000 eggs per day. **TRUE**
7. Bees can see the color red. **FALSE** (they see UV, blue, green — not red)
8. Honey bees visit 50-100 flowers in one foraging trip. **TRUE**
9. Bees sleep at night just like humans. **TRUE** (they do sleep, roughly 5-8 hours)
10. A bee's wings beat about 200 times per second. **TRUE**

**Round 3 — Bonus Challenge (5 points):**
11. "In ancient Egypt, bees were believed to be born from something. What?" (A: Sunlight, B: The tears of the sun god Ra, C: The Nile River, D: Desert sand) **Answer: B**

> "And that's our cultural connection! Ancient Egyptians believed bees were born from the tears of the sun god Ra. Bees were so sacred in Egypt that the pharaoh's royal title included the bee symbol. They used honey in medicine, in preserving mummies, and as offerings to the gods. Bees have been important to humans for thousands of years — across every culture on earth."

*Tally scores. Award the winning table a small privilege (first pick of trading cards, etc.).*

---

### CONNECT — "Welcome to the Hive" (10 minutes)

**[1:07 - 1:12] Virtual Hive Simulator Introduction**

*Pull up the Virtual Hive Simulator on the main screen.*

> "You passed Guard Gary's test. Welcome to the Honey Beez Hive. This is our Virtual Hive Simulator — you'll be using this throughout the program to track what you learn and watch your hive grow."

Walk through the basics:
- Show the hive cross-section view
- Point out the different bee roles visible in the animation
- Highlight the "Discoveries" log where new facts get recorded
- Show how completing sessions unlocks new hive features

*If individual devices are available, let participants explore for 3-4 minutes. If not, do a guided group exploration.*

**[1:12 - 1:15] Trading Cards and Bridge**

*Distribute Trading Cards Pack 1 to each participant.*

> "These are your Hive Character Cards. You've got Scout Sunny — that's our explorer. Nurse Nina — she takes care of the babies and the sick bees. Guard Gary — hive security. And Queen Bea herself — the heart of the colony. Keep these safe. We'll be adding to your collection every session."

*Read the bridge to Session 2:*

> "INCOMING TRANSMISSION: Recruits — you passed! Welcome to the hive. But here's the thing: knowing what a bee looks like is just the beginning. Next time, I'm going to teach you something that scientists didn't fully understand until 1967. I'm going to teach you how bees TALK. And trust me... it involves dancing. — Scout Sunny"

## Differentiation Tips

### For Participants Who Need More Support
- Provide pre-labeled anatomy diagrams they can color rather than label from scratch
- Pair with a stronger partner during the myth-busting activity
- Offer simplified trivia cards with only 2 answer choices instead of 4
- During the build phase, give them a specific small task (e.g., "You're in charge of making the two antennae") rather than a whole section
- Provide a word bank for any writing activities

### For Advanced Participants
- Challenge them to add internal anatomy to the bee model (honey stomach, brain, heart tube)
- Ask them to research one additional myth and create their own myth/fact card
- Have them calculate: if a bee visits 100 flowers per trip and makes 10 trips per day, how many flowers does one bee visit in a week?
- Encourage them to write an additional trivia question for the tournament
- Ask: "If bees have 5 eyes and humans have 2, what advantages and disadvantages might each arrangement have?"

### For Participants Who Finish Early
- Provide a "Bee Species Spotter" card with 10 common bee species to identify from photos
- Offer colored pencils and a blank card to design their own Honey Beez character
- Set up a "Bee Library" corner with age-appropriate bee books

## Take-Home Extension Activities

### Bee Detective Journal (Ongoing)
> "Your mission this week: become a Bee Detective. Go outside and observe. Can you spot any bees? Try to figure out: Is it a bee or a wasp? What color flowers is it visiting? How long does it stay on each flower? Draw or describe what you see in your journal. Bring your findings to next session."

### Kitchen Counter Experiment
> "Find honey in your kitchen. Put a tiny drop on a plate and look at it with a magnifying glass if you have one. Honey is made from flower nectar that has been processed inside the bee's honey stomach. Taste it — every batch of honey tastes different depending on which flowers the bees visited."

### Family Challenge
> "Teach someone at home THREE things you learned today about bees. Bonus points if you can convince them that bees have five eyes."

### Creative Extension
> "Draw your own version of what a bee sees. Remember — they can see ultraviolet light but NOT red. What would your bedroom look like through bee eyes?"

## Session Notes for Facilitator

- **Allergy Check:** At the start of the program, confirm whether any participants have bee sting allergies. This session is entirely indoors with no live bees, but awareness is important for future sessions.
- **Turmeric Warning:** Turmeric stains clothing and surfaces. Use smocks or old t-shirts during the pollen basket demonstration. Have wet wipes on hand.
- **Energy Management:** The session arc is designed to alternate between seated learning (Explore) and active creation (Make), with a high-energy game (Play) near the end. If energy is flagging during Explore, insert a quick "buzz break" — everyone stands, flaps their arms, and buzzes for 10 seconds.
- **Time Flexibility:** The Make section is the most flexible. If short on time, skip the presentation portion and have teams simply attach their sections. If you have extra time, let teams add creative details (flowers around the bee, a hive in the background, etc.).
