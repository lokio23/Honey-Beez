# Session 8: I Am a Bee Ambassador

**Honey Beez Curriculum | Ages 9-12 | 90 minutes**

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

- Synthesize knowledge from all seven previous sessions into a coherent understanding of why bees matter
- Create a one-sheet zine that communicates a bee-related message to a general audience
- Present a personal commitment to bee advocacy to a group (public speaking)
- Identify at least one ongoing action they will take as a Bee Ambassador beyond the program

---

## Facilitator Script

### HOOK -- "Look How Far You've Come" (10 minutes)

**[0:00-0:03] The Opening Statement**

Stand at the front of the room. Make eye contact with participants. Speak slowly and with genuine warmth.

> **Say:** "I want to tell you something, and I want you to really hear it. You now know more about bees than most adults on the planet. That's not an exaggeration. Most people can tell you that bees make honey and they sting. You can explain waggle dances, buzz pollination, optimal foraging theory, colony collapse disorder, phenological mismatch, and citizen science data collection. You've built bee hotels, played Nectar Economics, identified species by sight, and collected real scientific data. In eight sessions, you went from 'bees are cool' to 'I can explain the interconnected ecological crisis facing 20,000 pollinator species.' That is extraordinary."

Pause. Let them feel it.

**[0:03-0:07] The Grand Review**

> **Say:** "Let's take a speed tour of everything we've covered. I'm going to flash through our sessions, and I want you to shout out what you remember from each one."

Click through a slide for each session (or hold up a poster/card for each):

- **Session 1** (title/image) -- take 2-3 shout-outs
- **Session 2** (title/image) -- take 2-3 shout-outs
- **Session 3** (title/image) -- take 2-3 shout-outs
- **Session 4** (title/image) -- take 2-3 shout-outs
- **Session 5: Meet the Family** -- "How many bee species are there?" (20,000+!)
- **Session 6: Bees in Trouble** -- "Name a threat!" (4 Ps, climate, habitat)
- **Session 7: Bee Scientists** -- "What did we collect?" (Real data!)
- **Session 8: Today** -- "What are we becoming?" (Ambassadors!)

> **Say:** "The Honey Beez Hive characters have been with us the whole way. Scout Sunny taught us to explore. Nurse Nina taught us to care. Guard Gary taught us to protect. And Queen Bea taught us that every single bee -- every single person -- matters to the whole colony."

**[0:07-0:10] The Big Question**

> **Say:** "Today, we answer one question: What will YOU do with everything you know? Knowledge that stays in your head helps no one. Knowledge that you share changes the world. Today you become Bee Ambassadors, and your first mission is to create something that spreads what you've learned."

Hand out the review summary sheet for reference during zine-making.

---

### EXPLORE -- "The Full Picture" (20 minutes)

**[0:10-0:15] Connection Mapping**

> **Say:** "Before we make anything, let's connect the dots. Everything we've learned is linked."

On a large whiteboard or poster paper, draw a concept web together as a group. Start with "BEES" in the center.

> **Ask:** "What connects to bees?" As participants call out answers, draw connections:

Guide the web to include:
- Bees --> Pollination --> Food (1 in 3 bites)
- Bees --> Honey/Wax --> Human culture (Mayan stingless bees, Hopi traditions)
- Bees --> 20,000 species --> Solitary bees --> Bee hotels
- Bees --> Threats --> 4 Ps + Climate + Habitat
- Bees --> Colony communication --> Waggle dance --> Scientific discovery
- Bees --> Citizen science --> Data --> Protection
- Bees --> Us --> Ambassadors

The web should get visibly complex. That's the point.

> **Say:** "Look at this web. This is what you understand now. It's not just facts -- it's a system. Everything connects. When you pull on one thread, the whole web moves. That's ecology. That's why bees matter. Not just because they make honey. Because they're woven into everything."

**[0:15-0:20] Finding Your Message**

> **Say:** "Now, think about your assignment from last session. One thing you want to tell the world. It might be a fact that blew your mind. A species you fell in love with. A threat that makes you angry. A solution that gives you hope. There's no wrong answer. But it needs to be something you genuinely care about."

Give participants 3 minutes of quiet thinking time. Provide prompts on the board for anyone who's stuck:

- "The fact that surprised me most was..."
- "I want people to know about _____ bees because..."
- "The biggest thing people can do for bees is..."
- "I used to think _____ about bees, but now I know..."
- "If bees disappear, _____ would happen"

> **Say:** "Turn to the person next to you and share your message in one sentence. Practice saying it out loud."

Give pairs 2 minutes to share. This primes them for the zine and the presentation.

**[0:20-0:25] Zine Planning**

> **Say:** "You're going to turn your message into a zine -- a mini self-published booklet that you can hand to anyone. One sheet of paper, eight pages, your message, your art, your voice."

Hand out scratch paper for planning.

> **Say:** "Before we fold, let's plan. Your zine has 8 panels. Here's a suggested layout, but you can customize:"

Write on board:
1. **Cover:** Title + your name + art
2. **Page 1:** The hook -- a surprising fact to grab attention
3. **Page 2:** Why it matters -- connect bees to the reader's life
4. **Page 3:** The cool science -- one amazing thing about bees
5. **Page 4:** The problem -- one threat bees face
6. **Page 5:** The solution -- what people can do
7. **Page 6:** Your personal pledge as a Bee Ambassador
8. **Back cover:** Resources -- where to learn more (websites, books, citizen science platforms)

> **Say:** "Sketch a rough layout on your scratch paper first. You have 3 minutes to plan, then we fold and create."

---

### MAKE -- "Zine Creation Workshop" (25 minutes)

**[0:25-0:28] The One-Sheet Fold**

> **Say:** "Here's the magic of the one-sheet zine. One piece of paper becomes an 8-page booklet with one cut and a few folds. Watch carefully."

**Demonstrate the fold step by step:**

1. Fold the paper in half lengthwise (hot dog fold). Crease firmly. Unfold.
2. Fold the paper in half widthwise (hamburger fold). Crease firmly. Keep it folded.
3. Fold in half again widthwise. Crease. Unfold back to the single hamburger fold.
4. With the paper still in the hamburger fold, cut from the folded edge to the center crease (halfway across). Stop at the middle.
5. Unfold completely. You'll see a slit in the center of the paper.
6. Fold lengthwise again (hot dog fold).
7. Push the two ends toward the center -- the slit opens up and the pages form.
8. Flatten into a booklet shape. Crease all edges.

> **Say:** "Do it with me. Step by step."

Walk through each step with participants. Help anyone who gets stuck. Have a few pre-folded examples to show what the final product looks like.

> **Say:** "Open your booklet. Number the pages lightly in pencil: cover, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, back cover. Now you know where everything goes."

**[0:28-0:50] Creation Time**

> **Say:** "You have 22 minutes. Use your plan from the scratch paper. Fill every page. Use color. Make it something you'd be proud to hand to a stranger on the street. This is YOUR voice for bees."

Play background music (upbeat but not distracting).

**Facilitator circulates throughout, offering:**

- **Encouragement:** "That cover art is striking. The person who picks this up is going to want to read it."
- **Fact-checking support:** "You wrote that bees pollinate 75% of crops -- want to double-check that number on the review sheet? It's about 1 in 3 bites of food, or roughly 35% of crops by volume."
- **Design tips:** "If you make the title bigger and bolder, it'll catch the eye from further away."
- **Content guidance for stuck participants:** "What was your message? 'People should plant wildflowers.' Great -- so your hook might be: 'Your lawn is a food desert for bees.' Then explain why, and end with what to plant."
- **Spelling/grammar help** as requested (but don't over-correct -- zines are personal and imperfect by nature)

**Production tips to share during work time:**
- "Leave margins -- don't draw or write to the very edge of each panel"
- "Outline in black pen first, then color. It makes everything pop."
- "If you make a mistake, work it into the design. Zines are handmade -- imperfection is part of the charm."

**[0:50-0:52] Finishing Up**

> **Say:** "Two minutes to finish. Put your name on the back cover if you haven't already. If you want, add one thing to the back: a website, a hashtag, or a simple line like 'Learn more at your local library.' Give people a next step."

Collect zines (participants keep them after the gallery walk) and display on a table for viewing during the Play section.

---

### PLAY -- "Capstone Presentations + Gallery Walk" (15 minutes)

**[0:52-0:54] Setting the Stage**

Arrange chairs in a circle or semi-circle. Place zines on a display table in the center or along one wall.

> **Say:** "This is the Ambassador Ceremony. At the Honey Beez Hive, every new ambassador stands before the colony and makes a declaration: one thing they will do to be a voice for bees. This isn't a test. There are no wrong answers. It can be one sentence. It can be a paragraph. But it's YOUR commitment, spoken out loud, in front of your fellow ambassadors."

**[0:54-0:55] Modeling**

> **Say:** "I'll go first. My name is [your name], and as a Bee Ambassador, I commit to [your genuine personal commitment -- make this real, not performative]. For example: 'I commit to never using pesticides in my yard and to planting at least three native wildflower species this spring.'"

**[0:55-1:05] Presentations**

Go around the circle. Each participant stands (or stays seated if more comfortable) and shares:

1. Their name
2. One thing they learned that changed how they think
3. One thing they will DO as a Bee Ambassador

**Facilitator responses to each presentation:**
- Acknowledge specifically: "That's a powerful commitment. Planting milkweed will help both bees and butterflies."
- Connect to the curriculum: "You mentioned buzz pollination -- that was from Session 5. You remembered."
- Celebrate courage: "Thank you for standing up and sharing that. That took guts."

If any participant is too shy to present:
- Offer alternatives: "You can read from your zine if that's easier."
- "You can tell me privately and I'll share it for you."
- "You can just hold up your zine and show us the cover." Any level of participation counts.

**[1:05-1:07] Gallery Walk (Quick Version)**

> **Say:** "Take 2 minutes to walk around and look at everyone's zines. Read them. Appreciate them. If you see one that teaches you something you didn't know, tell that person."

Participants circulate, reading each other's zines. This is often a highlight -- kids are proud of what they made and excited to see what others created.

---

### CONNECT -- "Unlock, Celebrate, Graduate" (10 minutes)

**[1:07-1:09] Film Festival (If Applicable)**

If participants created stop-motion animations during the program:

> **Say:** "Before we graduate, we have a world premiere. The Honey Beez Film Festival."

Play each animation (they're typically 15-60 seconds). After each one, lead applause. After all have played, do a quick "audience choice" vote using paper slips.

Award certificates:
- Most Creative
- Best Science Communication
- Funniest
- Audience Choice

If no film festival, skip to the next section.

**[1:09-1:12] Full Site Unlock**

Open the digital platform on the projector.

> **Say:** "Since Session 1, you've been unlocking pieces of the Honey Beez digital world. Today, as graduating ambassadors, you get the whole thing. Every module. Every game. Every species explorer. Every citizen science tool. It's yours permanently. Your login works forever."

Walk through the unlocked features briefly:
- Full Species Explorer with all 50+ species
- Citizen Science Dashboard with data submission
- All session games and activities
- Ambassador community forum (moderated, safe space)
- Downloadable resources: field guides, trading cards, infographics

> **Say:** "This isn't the end. This is your home base. Come back to it whenever you want."

**[1:12-1:15] The Graduation Ceremony**

This is the emotional peak. Make it count.

> **Say:** "Will all Honey Beez graduates please stand."

Wait until everyone is standing.

> **Say:** "You came to this program as curious kids who liked bees. You're leaving as scientists, advocates, and ambassadors. You can identify species that most adults can't name. You've collected data that contributes to real research. You've built homes for wild bees. You understand threats that even some politicians don't fully grasp. And you've made a personal commitment to be a voice for 20,000 species that can't speak for themselves."

> **Say:** "Queen Bea says: 'The hive is not a building. The hive is every bee that carries our purpose in their heart.' You are part of the hive now."

Call each participant by name to receive their completion certificate. As they come up:
- Hand them the certificate (all 8 badge spaces filled)
- Shake their hand or offer a high-five
- Say something specific: "Ambassador [Name], congratulations."
- If you have small gifts (seed packets, pollinator plant starts), distribute them now

Lead group applause after all certificates are distributed.

**[1:15-1:17] The Bee Ambassador Pledge**

Hand out the Bee Ambassador Pledge cards.

> **Say:** "One last thing. Read this with me, out loud, together."

**The Bee Ambassador Pledge:**

> *I am a Bee Ambassador.*
> *I will speak for those who cannot speak for themselves.*
> *I will plant flowers where there are none.*
> *I will choose to protect, not to fear.*
> *I will share what I know with anyone who will listen.*
> *I will observe with care and record with honesty.*
> *I know that one person can make a difference,*
> *because one bee can pollinate a thousand flowers.*
> *The hive is bigger than any one of us.*
> *I carry its purpose with me.*

Read it together as a group. This often becomes a genuinely moving moment.

**[1:17-1:20] Celebration**

> **Say:** "That's it. You did it. Now let's celebrate."

Break out snacks, put on music, and let participants mingle, trade cards, compare zines, and enjoy the moment. Take group photos if parents/guardians have given media permission.

## Differentiation Tips

### For Participants Who Need More Support
- Provide a zine template with pre-drawn panel borders and light pencil prompts in each panel ("Your title goes here," "Draw a bee here," "Write one fact here")
- For the presentation, allow reading directly from the zine or from a pre-written index card
- Pair with a buddy for the gallery walk
- Offer a simplified pledge to read (fewer lines, simpler language)

### For Advanced Participants
- Challenge them to make a SECOND zine targeting a specific audience (e.g., one for adults, one for younger kids, one for local government)
- Invite them to give a longer presentation (2-3 minutes) incorporating visuals or demonstrations
- Ask them to create a "Bee Ambassador Action Plan" with specific, measurable goals for the next month
- Offer to connect them with local beekeeping associations, nature centers, or citizen science coordinators for ongoing involvement

### For Shy or Anxious Participants
- The presentation portion can be intimidating. Provide multiple levels of participation:
  - Level 1: Hold up your zine and show the cover (no speaking required)
  - Level 2: Read one sentence from your zine
  - Level 3: Share your ambassador commitment in your own words
  - Level 4: Full presentation with elaboration
- All levels are celebrated equally
- Offer the option to record a video statement instead of presenting live

### For Mixed-Age Groups
- Older participants can be "Senior Ambassadors" who help younger ones with zine construction
- Adjust presentation expectations by age (younger: 1-2 sentences; older: full commitment statement)
- Pair older and younger participants during the gallery walk for mentoring moments

## Take-Home Extension Activities

1. **Zine Distribution Challenge:** Make 5 copies of your zine (photocopy or hand-make more) and give them to 5 different people -- a family member, a teacher, a friend, a neighbor, and a stranger. Report back on their reactions.

2. **30-Day Bee Ambassador Journal:** For the next 30 days, do one bee-related thing each day. It can be small (observe a bee for 1 minute, water a plant, tell someone a fact) or big (plant a garden, give a presentation at school, write a letter to the editor). Journal each day.

3. **Teach a Session:** Using your zine and field guide, teach a mini "Bee 101" lesson to a younger sibling, cousin, or neighbor. Adapt what you learned to their level.

4. **Citizen Science Commitment:** Submit at least one observation per week to a citizen science platform for the next month. Track your contributions.

5. **Community Project:** Propose a bee-friendly project to your school, place of worship, or community center. Ideas: a pollinator garden, a "No Mow May" campaign, a bee hotel installation, a pesticide-free zone pledge. Use your zine as your proposal document.

6. **Letter Writing:** Write a letter to a local elected official about pollinator protection. Include at least 3 specific facts you learned in this program and one specific action you want them to take.
