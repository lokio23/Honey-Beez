# Session 2: "The Secret Language of Bees"

**Honey Beez Curriculum | Ages 9-12 | 80 minutes**

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

- Explain how the waggle dance encodes direction (angle relative to the sun), distance (duration of waggle run), and quality (vigor of the dance)
- Demonstrate the basic mechanics of a waggle dance to communicate a location
- Describe at least 2 other ways bees communicate (pheromones, piping, head-butting)
- Design a simple communication system that encodes location information without words

---

## Materials Needed

### Supplies
- Masking tape or painter's tape (for floor grid)
- Compass (or compass app on a phone for the facilitator)
- Paper plates (2 per participant)
- Markers and colored pencils
- Large poster paper (1 sheet per group of 4-5)
- Protractors (1 per group)
- String or yarn (assorted colors)
- Index cards (10 per participant)
- Small prizes or tokens for game winners (stickers, stamps, etc.)
- A flashlight or desk lamp (to represent "the sun")

### Printed Materials
- Waggle Dance Decoder reference sheet (1 per participant)
- Infographic #1 template: "How Bees Talk" (1 per participant)
- Bee Democracy fact sheet (1 per table)
- Communication System design worksheet (1 per participant)

### Technology
- Laptop/tablet with projector or large screen
- Video queued: real waggle dance footage (multiple examples available on YouTube from research labs — search for "honey bee waggle dance slow motion")
- Waggle Dance Simulator loaded and ready
- Speaker for audio

### Facilitator Prep
- Create a floor grid in the activity area using masking tape (a large circle with compass directions marked N/S/E/W, about 8 feet in diameter)
- Place a lamp or bright marker on one wall to represent "the sun"
- Hide 4-5 small "flower" tokens (colored paper flowers or objects) around the room before participants arrive — you will reveal these during the Play phase
- Prepare a simple waggle dance demonstration (practice the figure-eight movement)

## Narrative Through-Line

> Scout Sunny has an urgent problem. She's found an incredible patch of wildflowers — the best nectar source in miles. But she can't carry it all back herself. She needs to tell the other foragers EXACTLY where the flowers are. The problem? Bees can't talk. They can't text. They can't draw maps. So how does Sunny share her discovery? She DANCES. Today, we crack the code of the most sophisticated non-human communication system ever discovered.

## Facilitator Script

### HOOK — "The Dance That Stumped Scientists for 2,000 Years" (10 minutes)

**[0:00 - 0:03] The Mystery**

> "I want you to imagine something. You find the most amazing pizza place in the city. Best pizza you've ever tasted. You run home to tell your friends. But here's the catch: you cannot speak. You cannot write. You cannot point. You cannot draw. The ONLY thing you can do is... dance."

*Pause. Let participants react.*

> "Sounds impossible, right? But there's an animal that does this EVERY SINGLE DAY. And their directions are so accurate that their friends can find a tiny flower patch miles away, in a location they have NEVER been to before, based entirely on watching a 2-second dance."

**[0:03 - 0:07] Video Reveal**

*Play waggle dance footage. Play it once at normal speed, then once in slow motion.*

> "What you're watching is called the WAGGLE DANCE. This bee just returned from a flower patch. Watch — she runs in a straight line while waggling her abdomen back and forth, then circles back and does it again. A figure-eight, over and over."

> "Here's what's incredible: that straight-line waggle run is a CODE. The ANGLE of the run tells the other bees what DIRECTION to fly — relative to the sun. The DURATION of the run tells them how FAR to fly. And the INTENSITY — how hard she waggles — tells them how GOOD the food source is. It's like GPS, Yelp reviews, and a treasure map all encoded in a 2-second dance."

**[0:07 - 0:10] The Backstory**

> "A scientist named Karl von Frisch figured this out in the 1940s, but nobody believed him. Other scientists said he was crazy — that no insect brain could possibly encode that much information. It took until 1967 to prove him right. He won the Nobel Prize for it. The waggle dance is one of the most complex forms of communication in the entire animal kingdom outside of human language."

> "TRANSMISSION FROM SCOUT SUNNY: Recruits, I need your help. I've found the most incredible nectar source, but I need you to learn my language so we can coordinate. Today, I'm teaching you to speak Bee. Pay attention — your hive depends on it."

---

### EXPLORE — "Cracking the Waggle Code" (20 minutes)

**[0:10 - 0:16] Decoding Direction**

*Stand at the floor grid. Place the lamp/flashlight on one wall to represent the sun.*

> "OK, here's how direction works. Inside the hive, the comb hangs vertically. The bee dances on this vertical surface. STRAIGHT UP on the comb means 'fly toward the sun.' STRAIGHT DOWN means 'fly away from the sun.' Any other angle means 'fly at this angle relative to the sun.'"

*Demonstrate on the floor grid:*

1. Stand in the center. Point to the lamp. "The sun is over here."
2. "If I waggle-run straight toward the light — I'm saying 'fly directly toward the sun.'"
3. "If I waggle-run at a 45-degree angle to the right of the light — I'm saying 'fly 45 degrees to the right of the sun.'"
4. "If I waggle-run directly away from the light — I'm saying 'fly directly away from the sun.'"

*Have 2-3 volunteers try it. Give them a direction card (e.g., "The flowers are 90 degrees to the left of the sun") and see if they can orient their waggle run correctly.*

**[0:16 - 0:20] Decoding Distance**

> "Distance is even simpler. The LONGER the waggle run, the FARTHER the food. Scientists measured this precisely:"

*Write on board or display:*
- 1 second of waggling = approximately 1 kilometer (0.6 miles)
- A short buzz-buzz-buzz (less than a second) = the food is close, maybe 100-200 meters
- A long, sustained waggle run = the food could be several kilometers away

> "So if I do a quick waggle — bzzt — that means 'right nearby.' If I do a long waggle — bzzzzzzzzzzzt — 'it's a long flight, but worth it.'"

*Practice with participants: call out distances and have them waggle for the appropriate duration.*

**[0:20 - 0:24] Decoding Quality — And Bee Democracy**

> "But here's the part that really amazes scientists. The VIGOR of the dance tells the other bees how good the source is. If Scout Sunny finds an AMAZING flower patch, she dances like crazy — fast, enthusiastic, many repetitions. If it's just OK, she does a slower, shorter dance. The other bees can literally watch multiple dancers and compare reviews — like checking ratings on an app."

> "And this leads to something called BEE DEMOCRACY. When bees need to choose a new home — like when a swarm leaves the hive — scout bees fly out in all directions. They come back and dance about the locations they found. Scouts who found great spots dance hard. Scouts who found mediocre spots dance weakly. Other bees watch, then go check out the top-rated spots themselves. They come back and dance too."

> "And here's the wildest part: if a bee disagrees with another bee's choice, she HEAD-BUTTS her. Literally rams into the other dancer to tell her 'I disagree, stop promoting that location.' Eventually, through dancing, checking, and head-butting, the entire swarm reaches a consensus. The decision is usually the BEST available option. Bee democracy outperforms most human committees."

**[0:24 - 0:28] Other Bee Communication**

> "The waggle dance isn't the only way bees talk. Here are some others:"

- **Pheromones:** Chemical signals. The queen releases a pheromone that says "I'm alive, I'm healthy, everything is fine." If that pheromone stops, the workers immediately start raising a new queen. Alarm pheromone smells like bananas — if a bee stings you, other bees smell it and know there's a threat.
- **Piping:** Queens make a "piping" sound — a high-pitched vibration — to signal to rival queens still in their cells. It's basically a challenge call.
- **Tremble Dance:** A forager does a tremble dance when she comes back with so much nectar that there aren't enough house bees to process it. It means "We need more help in the kitchen!"
- **Stop Signal:** A bee vibrates her body against a dancer to say "Stop advertising that food source — I went there and it's dangerous (predator, etc.)."

**[0:28 - 0:30] Cultural Connection: The Melissae**

> "Bees and communication have fascinated humans for millennia. In ancient Greece, there were priestesses called the Melissae — which literally means 'the bees.' They served in the temple of the goddess Artemis and later at the Oracle of Delphi. The Greeks believed that bees carried messages between the human world and the divine world. The word 'melissa' still means 'honey bee' in Greek today. The idea that bees hold secret knowledge — that they have their own language — is ancient. And as we just learned, the ancients were right."

### MAKE — "Design Your Own Communication System" (25 minutes)

**[0:30 - 0:33] The Challenge Brief**

> "Scout Sunny says you understand HER language now. But here's her challenge: can you CREATE your own? Your team needs to design a communication system that can tell someone DIRECTION, DISTANCE, and QUALITY — without using any words or numbers."

*Distribute design worksheets and poster paper.*

**Rules:**
1. No spoken or written words
2. No pointing at the actual location
3. Must encode three things: direction, distance, and quality rating
4. Can use movement, sounds, symbols, colors, or any combination
5. Must be something another team could learn to read/use

**[0:33 - 0:50] Design Phase**

*Groups of 4-5 work together to design their system. Circulate and prompt:*

- "How are you encoding direction? What's your reference point — like the bees use the sun?"
- "How does someone reading your system know if the thing is far away or nearby?"
- "How do they know if it's a 5-star location or a 2-star location?"
- "Could someone who's never seen your system before figure it out? How would you teach them?"

**Example systems participants might invent:**
- A color-coded map system (red = far, blue = close; arrow direction; number of dots = quality)
- A clapping system (clap rhythm = distance, hand position = direction, volume = quality)
- A movement system (steps in a direction, speed = distance, jumping = quality rating)

**[0:50 - 0:55] Presentations**

*Each team has 60-90 seconds to demonstrate their system. One team member goes to a specific spot in the room. Without words, the "scout" uses their system to communicate that location to a "forager" teammate who has their eyes closed. The forager opens their eyes and tries to navigate to the correct spot.*

> "How close did they get? What worked? What was confusing?"

*After all teams present:*

> "Notice how every team solved the same problem differently — just like different species of bees use different dances. Honey bees waggle. Stingless bees use a system of scent trails. Bumblebees use a simpler 'run excitedly around the nest' approach. There are many ways to solve the communication problem."

### PLAY — "The Waggle Dance Challenge" (15 minutes)

**[0:55 - 0:57] Setup**

> "Guard Gary has hidden nectar caches around the room. Scout Sunny found them and she needs to tell you where they are — using the waggle dance. Here's how this works."

*Reveal the floor grid. Remind everyone: the lamp = the sun.*

**Rules:**
1. One "scout" from each team will see the secret location of a hidden flower token
2. The scout returns to their team and performs a waggle dance (angle relative to the "sun" for direction, duration for distance)
3. The team interprets the dance and sends a "forager" to retrieve the flower
4. Closest team to the actual flower location wins the round
5. Rotate scouts each round

**[0:57 - 1:07] Game Rounds**

*Run 4-5 rounds, increasing difficulty:*

- **Round 1:** Flower is in a simple direction (directly toward or away from the sun), close distance
- **Round 2:** Flower is at a 45-degree angle, medium distance
- **Round 3:** Flower is at a tricky angle (e.g., 120 degrees left of sun), far distance
- **Round 4:** Add a "quality" component — the scout must also convey quality. High quality = the flower has a bonus sticker attached. Low quality = no bonus.
- **Round 5 (Bonus):** Two scouts dance at the same time about two different flower locations. The team must decide which one to send a forager to first based on which dance is more vigorous (better quality).

*Award points for accuracy. Extra points for Round 5 if they choose the higher-quality flower.*

> "This is EXACTLY what happens inside a real hive. Multiple scouts dance at once. Foragers have to watch, compare, and make a decision. You just experienced bee decision-making firsthand."

Trust me — you've never seen anything like it. Also, she'll be teaching you how to make something that could save our species. No pressure. — Scout Sunny"

## Differentiation Tips

### For Participants Who Need More Support
- Provide a pre-drawn waggle dance diagram they can annotate rather than drawing from scratch
- During the Waggle Dance Challenge, allow these participants to be the "forager" (interpreting) rather than the "scout" (encoding), which is harder
- Offer a simplified communication system template with fill-in-the-blank options: "For direction, we will use ___. For distance, we will use ___."
- Pair with a supportive partner during the design phase
- Provide the Waggle Dance Decoder sheet with visual step-by-step instructions

### For Advanced Participants
- Challenge them to calculate: "If 1 second of waggle = 1 kilometer, and the flowers are 3.5 km away at a 72-degree angle from the sun, how would you dance this?"
- Ask them to research: "The sun moves across the sky during the day. How do bees adjust their dances to account for this?" (Answer: they update the angle over time — truly remarkable)
- Have them design a communication system that also encodes a TIME dimension (e.g., "the flowers are best in the morning")
- Encourage them to think about failure modes: "What happens if a bee's dance is slightly wrong? How much error can the system tolerate?"

### For Participants Who Finish Early
- Provide a "Dance Decoder" puzzle sheet with 10 waggle dances to interpret
- Offer materials to create a waggle dance flip-book animation
- Let them explore the Waggle Dance Simulator independently

## Take-Home Extension Activities

### Communication Challenge
> "Tonight, try to communicate the location of something in your house to a family member using ONLY movement — no words, no pointing, no writing. See how close they get. You just did what a bee does every day."

### Observation Mission
> "Next time you're outside and you see a bee on a flower, watch it closely. Which direction does it fly when it leaves? Can you figure out which direction its hive might be? Forager bees usually fly in a straight line back to the hive — it's called a 'beeline.' That's where the expression comes from."

### Research Extension
> "Look up 'stingless bee communication.' These bees live in tropical areas and they DON'T do the waggle dance. Instead, they leave scent trails from the food source back to the hive — like a chemical breadcrumb trail. How does this compare to the waggle dance? What are the advantages and disadvantages of each system?"

### Creative Extension
> "Create a comic strip (at least 4 panels) showing Scout Sunny discovering an amazing flower patch and communicating its location back to the hive through her waggle dance. Show the other bees interpreting the dance and flying to the flowers."

## Session Notes for Facilitator

- **Space Requirements:** The Waggle Dance Challenge requires open floor space. If your room is small, use a hallway or outdoor space. The floor grid should be at least 8 feet in diameter for participants to move comfortably.
- **Sun Position:** The "sun" (lamp) should stay in the SAME position throughout the session. If you move it, the entire direction system changes — which is actually a great teaching moment if it happens accidentally.
- **Common Misconception:** Participants often think the waggle dance points directly at the food source horizontally. Emphasize that on the vertical comb, UP = toward the sun, and the angle is relative to that. This is the trickiest concept in the session.
- **Energy Note:** This session is highly active. The Play section is physically engaging (dancing, moving around the room). If you have participants with mobility limitations, they can serve as "navigators" who interpret the dance and verbally guide the forager.
- **Video Backup:** If the waggle dance video doesn't load, have a clear diagram ready and demonstrate the dance yourself. Exaggerate the figure-eight motion and the waggle of the abdomen. Participants find live demonstration very engaging.
